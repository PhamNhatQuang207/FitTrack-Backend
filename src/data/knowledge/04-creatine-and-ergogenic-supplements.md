# Creatine Supplementation Safety, Dosage, and Efficacy

## Efficacy and Form
According to the 2017 ISSN Position Stand on Creatine Supplementation (Kreider et al., 2017):
- **Creatine monohydrate** is the most extensively studied and clinically effective nutritional supplement available for increasing high-intensity exercise capacity and lean body mass during training.
- Raising intramuscular phosphocreatine stores improves performance in repeated short-duration, high-intensity bouts (such as resistance training sets and sprints), supporting greater training volume and faster between-set recovery.
- Alternative forms of creatine have not been consistently shown to outperform standard creatine monohydrate in safety, bioavailability, or efficacy.

## Dosing Protocols
Two evidence-based protocols saturate muscle creatine stores:
1. **Rapid Loading Protocol:** Consume approximately **0.3 g/kg body weight/day** (commonly ~20 g/day split into four 5 g doses) for **3 to 5 days** (up to 5–7 days), followed by a daily maintenance dose of **3 to 5 g/day** (or ~0.03 g/kg/day).
2. **Steady Daily Dosing (No Loading):** Taking **3 to 5 g/day** consistently without a loading phase achieves full muscle saturation over roughly **3 to 4 weeks**, while minimizing the mild gastrointestinal discomfort or rapid water-weight shift some people notice during loading. (Even lower intakes of 2–3 g/day elevate stores over 3–4 weeks, though 3–5 g/day is standard for athletes).

## Safety, Timing, and Practical Notes
- Long-term studies (up to several years) in healthy populations show creatine monohydrate at recommended dosages is safe and well-tolerated, without causing kidney damage in individuals with healthy renal function.
- **Weight Gain Note:** Initial weight increases (~0.5–1.5 kg) during saturation reflect intracellular water retention inside muscle cells rather than body fat gain.
- **Consistency Over Timing:** Daily consistency matters far more than exact timing, though taking creatine near a workout or with a carbohydrate/protein-containing meal may modestly support uptake.
- Individuals with pre-existing kidney disease or on nephrotoxic medications should consult a physician before starting supplementation.
