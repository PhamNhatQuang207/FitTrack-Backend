# Protein and Macronutrient Guidelines for Resistance Training

## Daily Protein Targets
Based on the 2017 International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) Position Stand on Protein and Exercise (Jäger et al., 2017):
- **General Muscle Building and Maintenance:** For most exercising individuals aiming to build or maintain muscle mass, an overall daily protein intake of **1.4 to 2.0 g/kg body weight/day** is sufficient.
- **Fat Loss / Caloric Deficit:** During hypocaloric periods, resistance-trained individuals may require higher protein intakes of **2.3 to 3.1 g/kg/day** (especially when already lean) to maximize retention of lean body mass.
- **High-Protein Body Composition Effects:** Evidence indicates intakes above **3.0 g/kg/day** may support further favorable body composition changes (fat loss promotion) in resistance-trained individuals without known harm in healthy kidneys.

## Per-Meal Protein Dosing and Timing
- To maximally stimulate muscle protein synthesis (MPS), aim for **~0.25 g of high-quality protein per kg of body weight per meal**, or an absolute dose of **20 to 40 g per serving**.
- Distribute protein feedings relatively evenly throughout the day, roughly every **3 to 4 hours**, including a dose containing sufficient essential amino acids (especially leucine, ~2–3 g) around training sessions.
- Total daily protein intake remains the primary driver; peri-workout timing provides an incremental optimization once daily intake is met.

## Protein Quality and Food Sources
- Whole-food protein sources with complete essential amino acid profiles and high digestibility include lean meats, poultry, fish, eggs, dairy (milk, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese), and soy/tofu.
- Plant-based lifters can meet identical MPS targets by combining complementary plant protein sources (legumes, grains, soy, seitan) or increasing per-meal portions slightly to hit the leucine threshold.
- Protein supplements (such as whey, casein, or soy/pea isolate) are convenient, evidence-backed tools to close daily protein gaps rather than mandatory replacements for whole food.

## Carbohydrates and Dietary Fats in Training Diets
- **Carbohydrates** serve as the primary high-output fuel for resistance training volume and anaerobic glycolytic work. Higher training volumes and multi-session days benefit from adequate carbohydrate availability around workouts to preserve session intensity and glycogen stores.
- **Dietary Fats** support hormone production, fat-soluble vitamin absorption, and cellular health. After setting daily protein targets and minimum essential dietary fat intake, remaining daily calories can be allocated flexibly between carbohydrates and fats based on training output and food preference.
