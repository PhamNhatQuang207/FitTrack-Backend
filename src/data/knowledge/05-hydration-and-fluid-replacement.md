# Hydration and Fluid Replacement for Exercise Performance

## Why Hydration Matters
Per the American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) Position Stand on Exercise and Fluid Replacement (Sawka et al., 2007):
- Dehydration exceeding **>2% of body weight** impairs aerobic endurance performance and increases thermal strain, cardiovascular drift, and perceived exertion—especially in warm or humid gym environments.
- Even in indoor strength training, starting a session hypohydrated can increase fatigue and reduce work capacity across high-volume sessions.

## Pre-Exercise Hydration
- Begin training in a euhydrated (normally hydrated) state with normal plasma electrolyte levels.
- Practical pre-hydration guideline: slowly consume approximately **5 to 7 mL of fluid per kg of body weight** at least **4 hours before exercise**. If urine remains dark or minimal, add another **~3 to 5 mL/kg** about **2 hours prior** to training.

## During-Exercise Fluid and Electrolyte Replacement
- Sweat rates vary dramatically across individuals, clothing, and room temperature (often 0.5 to 2.0+ L/hour). Rather than relying on a fixed universal volume, aim to **prevent >2% body-weight loss** from sweat during the session without overdrinking so much that body weight increases.
- For typical 45–75 minute indoor gym sessions, drinking water to thirst or taking small periodic sips between sets is usually sufficient.
- For **prolonged exercise (>1–2 hours)** or heavy salty sweaters, including **sodium** (and carbohydrates when fueling endurance/high-volume work) helps stimulate thirst, retain fluid, and avoid exercise-associated hyponatremia caused by excessive plain-water intake.

## Post-Exercise Rehydration
- If rapid recovery is needed before another training session within 12–24 hours, consume approximately **1.25 to 1.5 L of fluid per kg of body weight lost** during exercise (since urinary losses continue during rehydration), paired with sodium-containing meals or drinks to restore fluid balance effectively.
