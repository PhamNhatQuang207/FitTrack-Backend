# Autoregulation (RPE/RIR), Sleep Recovery, Deloading, and Safety Boundaries

## Repetitions in Reserve (RIR) and RPE Autoregulation
Zourdos et al. (2016) validated a resistance-training-specific **Rating of Perceived Exertion (RPE) scale anchored to Repetitions in Reserve (RIR)**:
- **RPE 10 (0 RIR):** Maximum effort; zero additional repetitions possible with acceptable form.
- **RPE 9 (1 RIR):** Could have performed 1 more repetition.
- **RPE 8 (2 RIR):** Could have performed 2 more repetitions.
- **RPE 7 (3 RIR):** Could have performed 3 more repetitions.
- **Practical Application:** Performing most hypertrophy working sets at **RPE 7–9 (1–3 RIR)** captures high mechanical tension and motor unit recruitment while managing systemic fatigue and preserving form across multi-set workouts. Reserve RPE 9.5–10 selectively for low-skill isolation movements or safe peak testing rather than every heavy barbell compound set.

## Sleep and Recovery Requirements
According to the Joint Consensus Statement of the American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) and Sleep Research Society (Watson et al., 2015):
- Healthy adults should regularly obtain **7 or more hours of sleep per night** (typically **7–9 hours**) to support optimal health, cognitive function, metabolic regulation, and physical recovery.
- Chronic sleep restriction (< 7 hours/night) impairs recovery, increases perceived exertion, degrades hormonal/metabolic health, and raises injury and illness risk.

## Deloading and Fatigue Management
- Over weeks of progressive overload, accumulated joint and central nervous system fatigue can mask fitness gains.
- Signs a planned **deload week** (or 4–7 days of reduced volume/load) may be warranted include persistent performance regression across multiple sessions, unusual joint/tendon soreness, poor sleep quality, and elevated resting fatigue.
- Typical deload approach: maintain movement patterns while reducing working sets by **30–50%** and keeping effort further from failure (**3–4+ RIR**).

## Medical and Injury Safety Boundaries
- Distinguish normal **muscle soreness (DOMS)**—diffuse ache peaking 24–72 hours post-workout that eases with light warm-up—from **warning pain** (sharp, localized joint/tendon pain, radiating numbness/tingling, swelling, or sudden mechanical catching).
- Never push heavy loads through sharp joint or lower-back pain. Stop the aggravating movement, substitute a pain-free range or machine variation only if completely pain-free, and consult a qualified physician or physical therapist for persistent, worsening, or acute injuries.
- AI coaching guidance is educational and does not replace medical diagnosis or individualized clinical rehabilitation.
